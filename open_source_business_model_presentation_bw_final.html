These companies are built around open-source software, yet they generate substantial revenue. None of them sells the underlying code as its main product. Their revenue comes from commercial offerings built around the software.

Bridge: “None of these companies sells the code itself. So what exactly are they selling?”

Definition

What does “open source” mean?

  • Source code is publicly available
  • Users can inspect, modify, and redistribute it
  • Distributed under an open-source license
  • “Free” means freedom—not necessarily zero cost

Open Source as a Business Model3 / 15

Speaker notes

Open source means that source code is available under a license granting important freedoms: to inspect, modify, and redistribute the software. “Free” refers primarily to freedom, not necessarily to a zero price. Companies can charge for products and services built around open-source software.

Bridge: “But if anyone can use, modify, and share the software, where is the business opportunity?”

Key idea

Companies do not sell the code.

They sell what surrounds the code:

  • Support
  • Reliability
  • Convenience
  • Enterprise features
  • Hosting
  • Legal certainty
  • Ecosystem access
Open Source as a Business Model4 / 15

Speaker notes

The business opportunity is not usually in selling the code itself. Instead, companies reduce friction for customers: they help them deploy, operate, secure, scale, integrate, and comply with software requirements. The commercial offer surrounds the freely available technology.

Open Source as a Business Model5 / 15

Speaker notes

These models are not always completely separate in practice; companies often combine several. Each has a different main answer to the question: “What is the customer paying for?”

Bridge: “For each model, we will ask the same question: what exactly does the customer pay for?”

Model 1

1. Support and subscription

Imagine: a free app with a paid premium support plan.
  • Software is free
  • Company sells expertise and reliability
  • Support, updates, security, training
  • Recurring enterprise subscription

Open Source as a Business Model6 / 15

Speaker notes

Support can be sold as a service, while a subscription productizes that support into a recurring, standardized offering. Red Hat combines both: it sells enterprise support, certified software, and subscriptions around open-source technologies.

Bridge: “Support solves operational risk. But what happens when customers need features that the free version does not provide?”

Model 2

2. Open core

Imagine: a free basic house; pay for extra rooms and a garage.
  • Free open-source core
  • Paid proprietary additions
  • Enterprise, security, compliance, and scale features
  • Community edition and commercial edition

Open Source as a Business Model7 / 15

Speaker notes

The open-source version is useful, but the commercial version adds capabilities that matter especially to larger organizations. GitLab is an example of this approach: its free core is complemented by paid Premium and Ultimate tiers.

Bridge: “Open core limits functionality. Dual licensing limits the legal terms of use.”

Model 3

3. Dual licensing

Imagine: a free photo with attribution; pay to use it commercially without restrictions.
  • Same software, two licenses
  • Open-source license: free under conditions
  • Commercial license: paid alternative terms
  • Company sells legal certainty

Open Source as a Business Model8 / 15

Speaker notes

The company may offer the same or very similar software under different licenses. Customers pay when they need licensing terms that fit their commercial use case. The commercial license provides legal certainty and flexibility.

Bridge: “But many customers do not want to operate software themselves. They want someone else to run it.”

Model 4

4. Managed cloud / SaaS

Imagine: a free washing machine at home; pay per wash at a laundromat.
  • Company hosts the software
  • Customer pays for convenience
  • Hosting, backups, upgrades, monitoring, scaling
  • Subscription or usage-based pricing

Open Source as a Business Model9 / 15

Speaker notes

Customers are not paying for the software code; they are paying for someone else to run it reliably. MongoDB Atlas is a managed database service built around MongoDB’s database technology.

Bridge: “SaaS monetizes operations. A platform monetizes the ecosystem around the software.”

Model 5

5. Platform and ecosystem

Imagine: a free WordPress website; pay for a premium theme or plugin.
  • Open technology attracts users and partners
  • Company monetizes the surrounding platform
  • Cloud usage, enterprise features, integrations, partners
  • Value grows with the ecosystem

The open-source technology creates adoption and a community. The company then monetizes the commercial platform, managed cloud service, enterprise features, and ecosystem around it. Confluent is built around Apache Kafka.

Open Source as a Business Model13 / 15

Speaker notes

Open source creates widespread adoption and trust. Companies then earn revenue by removing the operational and commercial friction that organizations face when using software in production.

Bridge: “So the real product is not the code. It is the outcome the customer gets from using it.”

Closing

  • But the removal of friction:
